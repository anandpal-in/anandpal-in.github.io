Science and technology do more than produce knowledge and tools. They unsettle old certainties, reshape institutions, and change what societies accept as true, possible, and right. My work begins in the physics laboratory and extends to the questions that follow from it: how knowledge is made, how it should be taught, and how it ought to shape public life.

Science

Science begins with curiosity, the ability to ask questions, the recognition of causality beyond correlations, and an understanding of why nature behaves as it does. My research explores the hidden order of quantum materials, from superconductivity and magnetism to materials for energy applications. Over time, this pursuit has also drawn me to broader questions: what makes scientific knowledge distinctive, how science interacts with tradition, and how we know what we claim to know.

Education

Civilization advances by building on knowledge passed down through generations. That is what makes education so vital, because it is not merely about transferring information; it is also about character building and shaping how we think, question, and engage with the world. With rapidly changing technology, the role of education in nurturing curiosity, critical thinking, scientific temper, and character becomes ever more important.

Teaching → Writing →

Society

Every scientific and technological advance unsettles something in society: an assumption, a tradition, or an established way of life. These changes raise questions about the relationship between science and tradition, the consequences of innovation, and how science shapes our understanding of the world. In an age of post-truth narratives and widespread misinformation, understanding how doubt is cultivated and scientific evidence challenged has become increasingly important.
